# Verified winner inspiration for item 25: Natasha Kulviwat

The owner's September 26, 2026 request explicitly names Natasha's prior ISEF project as inspiration, not as a license to invent results. The [Society for Science 2023 ISEF abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?AllAbstracts=False&Category=Biomedical+and+Health+Sciences&FairCountry=Any+Country&FairState=Any+State&ISEFYears=0%2C&ProjectId=23691) identifies Natasha Kulviwat's project as "The Neurobiology of Suicide: Claudin-5 Is a Novel Biomarker of Suicide Pathogenesis." The [official 2023 ISEF media kit](https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-isef-2023-media-kit/) reports that she analyzed de-identified brain tissue from 20 people, measured cytokine and claudin-5 protein levels, and received the Gordon E. Moore Award. The [Society for Science 2024 STS profile](https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2024-student-finalists/natasha-kulviwat/) describes a later related study of claudin-5, albumin, immunoglobulin G and regulatory genes. The later STS work is not automatically part of the 2023 ISEF experiment; preserve the distinction.

**Transferable design pattern, not a same-task comparator:** name a biologically meaningful mechanism, use human specimens with documented phenotype, triangulate different measurement types and controls where available, and state what each measurement can and cannot establish. For the ten disease projects, ask judges what clinical phenotype, independent specimen validation, orthogonal protein/functional evidence and alternative explanations are missing. Public-GEO-only transcript associations cannot be presented as equivalent to new protein measurements or causal tests.

This winner does not provide a PPD same-task benchmark, prove any candidate disease biomarker, or substitute for an independent cohort, 40 genuine external services, 120 accession records, 50 substantive pages, or ten critique rounds per disease.
